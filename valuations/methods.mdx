## Methodology Overview

The Attention Valuation methodology combines quantitative and qualitative factors to provide a comprehensive measure of organizational value in the attention economy.

### Core Components

The valuation model consists of three primary components:

1. **Sales Value** - Direct revenue from merchandise, matchday, and player sales
2. **Media Value** - Calculated value from media exposure and engagement across channels
3. **Brand Multiplier** - Qualitative factor based on brand strength (1.0 - 10.0)

These components work together to create the Total Attention Valuation, which represents the complete value of your organization's attention and reach.

### Philosophy

Modern organizations derive value not only from direct revenue but also from the **attention they command** across various channels. The Brand Multiplier acknowledges that strong brands can **leverage attention more effectively**, creating a compounding effect on overall valuation.

## Total Attention Valuation

The primary metric that combines all value components to provide a comprehensive measure of your organization's worth.

**Components:**

- Sales Value (direct revenue)
- Media Value (value from all media channels)
- Brand Multiplier (qualitative brand strength factor)

The Brand Multiplier amplifies the combined Sales and Media Value, reflecting how brand strength influences overall valuation.

## Total Reach

Aggregate measure of audience across all channels, combining:

- **TV Viewers** - Total television audience
- **Web TV Views** - Online streaming views
- **PR Reach** - Press and media coverage reach
- **Digital Impressions** - Digital advertising impressions
- **Social Impressions** - Number of people who saw social media posts
- **Attendance** - Physical event attendance
- **Hospitality Guests** - VIP and hospitality attendees

<Note>
  **What's NOT Included**: Social Followers and Social Engagements are NOT included in Total Reach.
  Followers represent potential audience (not actual reach), while Engagements are already counted
  within Social Impressions (anyone who engaged also saw the post).
</Note>

## Sales Value

Direct revenue calculation combining:

- **Merchandise Revenue** - Total sales of club merchandise
- **Matchday Revenue** - Ticket sales and matchday income
- **Player Sales Value** - Cumulative transfer fees from player sales

<Info>Sales values are entered directly - no CPM calculation involved for these components.</Info>

## Media Value

Calculated value from media exposure and engagement across multiple channels:

- **TV Value** - Television exposure value
- **Web TV Value** - Online streaming value
- **PR Value** - Press and media coverage value
- **Digital Value** - Digital advertising value
- **Social Value** - Social media value (followers, impressions, engagements)
- **Attendance Value** - Physical event attendance value
- **Hospitality Value** - VIP and hospitality guest value

### CPM-Based Calculation

Most media components use CPM (Cost Per Mille/Thousand) methodology to calculate value. CPM represents the market value of exposure - what that reach would cost to purchase as advertising.

<Tip>
  **CPM = Cost Per Mille**: CPM is the industry standard metric measuring the cost of 1,000
  impressions. This methodology is recognized globally by marketing professionals, sponsors, and
  stakeholders.
</Tip>

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is CPM methodology and how does it work?

CPM (Cost Per Mille) methodology calculates the cost of reaching 1,000 impressions. In valuation, we use industry-standard CPM rates to determine the advertising value of your organic media exposure. This represents what sponsors would pay for equivalent reach.

### Why use CPM instead of actual costs for valuation?

CPM represents the market value of exposure, not your actual spending. If you get 1 million TV viewers organically, the CPM calculation shows what that exposure would cost to buy as advertising - this is the value you're providing to sponsors and what they would pay for equivalent reach.

### How are calculations standardized across industries?

The Attention Valuation methodology follows industry-standard advertising metrics used globally. CPM-based calculations, brand multiplier concepts, and reach aggregation align with practices recognized by marketing professionals, sponsors, and stakeholders worldwide, ensuring credibility and acceptance.

### Why does brand strength multiply the total valuation?

Strong brands leverage attention more effectively. A post from a well-known organization gets more engagement than the same post from a lesser-known entity. The brand multiplier methodology reflects this brand amplification effect on attention value - stronger brands create more value from the same exposure.

### Can I customize CPM rates for my market?

Yes! CPM rates should reflect your specific geographic market, industry sector, and audience demographics. Markets like Denmark, US, and UK have different CPM standards. Consult with media buyers or industry reports to use accurate rates for credible valuations.

### What metrics are included in the brand multiplier?

The brand multiplier uses four metrics on a 1-10 scale: Popularity (brand awareness), CSR (corporate social responsibility), Culture (brand identity strength), and Fans/Loyalty. These scores combine to create the multiplier that amplifies your sales and media value.

### What if I don't have all data for calculations?

Enter zero for missing components. The methodology calculates value only from available data. However, more complete data equals more accurate valuation for sponsor proposals. Focus on collecting data for your highest-value channels first.

### How do industry standards affect valuation accuracy?

Using industry-standard CPM methodology ensures your valuations are recognized and trusted by sponsors, investors, and stakeholders. It provides a common language for discussing sponsorship value and makes your proposals more credible and defensible.

### How often is methodology updated?

The methodology is reviewed and updated periodically to reflect current media market conditions and industry best practices. Using in-app documentation ensures you always have access to the most current formulas and guidance.
