# Negotiator Mode & Deal Structuring

**Configure negotiator mode to optimize sponsorship pricing negotiations using cost-plus methodology.** Define custom coverage percentage labels, hide unit costs from proposals, and focus on contribution margin ratios to structure profitable deals while maintaining pricing flexibility and transparency with sponsors.

## What is Negotiator Mode?

Negotiator mode is a specialized Package Builder view that transforms how you present pricing to sponsors by hiding unit costs and emphasizing coverage percentages instead.

**Purpose**: Define custom labels for different coverage percentage ranges to guide pricing negotiations.

**Key Benefits**:
- **Cost-plus pricing**: Calculate margins based on actual costs
- **Hidden costs**: Keep unit costs confidential in proposals
- **Margin visibility**: Focus on contribution margin ratios
- **Flexible pricing**: Adjust pricing without revealing cost structure
- **Professional presentation**: Clean proposals without cost details

<Info>
**Negotiator Mode**: A special view mode in Package Builder that hides unit costs and focuses on coverage percentages. Useful for proposal-based pricing strategies and margin-focused deal structuring.
</Info>

## Default Coverage Labels

**Default Labels:**
- **0-25%**: "Very Low Coverage" (below cost, loss-making)
- **25-50%**: "Low Coverage" (minimal margin, break-even range)
- **50-75%**: "Medium Coverage" (moderate profitability)
- **75-100%**: "High Coverage" (strong margins, target range)
- **100%+**: "Full Coverage" (maximum profitability, premium pricing)

## How Negotiator Mode Works

### Cost-Plus Pricing Methodology

**Step 1: Define Unit Costs**
- Set actual cost for each inventory item
- Include direct costs (production, delivery, staff)
- Add indirect costs (overhead, admin)
- Calculate total item cost

**Step 2: Set Sales Price**
- Add desired margin to unit cost
- Calculate coverage percentage automatically
- View margin in both percentage and currency
- Adjust pricing to target margin range

**Step 3: Review Coverage Labels**
- System shows coverage label based on percentage
- "High Coverage" indicates strong margins
- "Low Coverage" signals pricing review needed
- Labels guide pricing optimization

### Hidden Costs in Proposals

When negotiator mode is enabled:

**What Sponsors See:**
- Package name and tier
- Inclusions with quantities
- Total sales price
- Terms and conditions
- **NO unit costs displayed**

**What You See in Builder:**
- All unit costs visible
- Coverage percentage for each item
- Total package margin
- Coverage labels guiding decisions
- Cost-plus calculations

**Benefits:**
- Protects cost structure confidentiality
- Enables flexible pricing negotiations
- Maintains professional presentation
- Focuses discussion on value, not costs
- Prevents sponsor cost comparisons

## Configuration Steps

### Accessing Negotiator Settings

1. Navigate to **Sponsorships** → **Inventory**
2. Click **Settings** tab
3. Select **Negotiator** section
4. View coverage range labels

### Editing Coverage Labels

1. Click **Edit** button next to coverage range
2. Enter new label text (max 50 characters)
3. Use terminology your team understands
4. Translate to your organization's language
5. Click **Save** to apply changes

### Testing Changes

1. Go to Package Builder
2. Enable Negotiator Mode toggle
3. Add items to package
4. Verify coverage labels display correctly
5. Generate proposal preview
6. Confirm unit costs are hidden

<Tip>
**Language Consistency**: Use labels that match your organization's internal pricing terminology. If your team uses "Contribution Margin" instead of "Coverage," update labels accordingly.
</Tip>

## Best Practices

### Margin Target Guidelines

**Loss Prevention (0-25%)**:
- Avoid unless strategic partnership
- Requires senior approval
- Document rationale clearly
- Monitor closely for value exchange

**Break-Even Range (25-50%)**:
- Acceptable for high-value sponsors
- Include additional benefits/exposure
- Ensure long-term relationship value
- Balance portfolio with higher-margin deals

**Target Range (50-75%)**:
- Ideal profitability zone
- Sustainable business model
- Covers all costs plus reasonable profit
- Most packages should target this range

**Premium Range (75-100%+)**:
- Exceptional opportunities
- Highly desirable sponsors
- Limited availability items
- Justifiable premium pricing

### Deal Structuring Strategies

**Value-Based Pricing**:
- Focus on sponsor benefits, not costs
- Emphasize ROI potential
- Highlight exclusivity and scarcity
- Use negotiator mode to avoid cost discussions

**Tiered Pricing**:
- Higher tiers justify higher margins
- Platinum sponsors expect premium pricing
- Lower tiers may require competitive margins
- Use coverage labels to maintain consistency

**Package Bundling**:
- Combine high and moderate margin items
- Average coverage percentage across bundle
- Create perceived value through bundling
- Maintain overall target margin

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is cost-plus pricing in sponsorships?

Cost-plus pricing calculates sponsorship package prices by adding a desired profit margin to the actual unit costs of inventory items. This methodology ensures profitability, maintains consistent margin ratios, accounts for all direct and indirect costs, and provides transparent internal pricing structure for decision-making.

### How do I calculate coverage percentage?

Coverage percentage is calculated as (Sales Price - Unit Cost) ÷ Sales Price × 100. For example, if an item costs $100 and sells for $200, the coverage is ($200 - $100) ÷ $200 × 100 = 50%. This represents the margin as a percentage of sales price.

### Why hide unit costs from sponsors?

Hiding unit costs protects your pricing strategy confidentiality, prevents sponsors from negotiating based on costs, focuses discussions on value and benefits, avoids comparisons between different sponsors, and maintains pricing flexibility for future negotiations without revealing internal cost structure.

### Can I use different labels for each package?

No, coverage labels are organization-wide settings that apply to all packages in negotiator mode. All packages use the same coverage percentage ranges and labels. This ensures consistency in pricing terminology, standardizes margin targets across teams, and simplifies training and communication.

### What's the difference between margin and coverage?

Margin is the difference between sales price and cost (Sales Price - Cost), shown in currency. Coverage percentage is the margin expressed as a percentage of sales price ((Sales Price - Cost) ÷ Sales Price × 100). Coverage is more useful for comparing profitability across different price points.

### How do I enable negotiator mode?

Enable negotiator mode in Package Builder by toggling the "Negotiator Mode" switch. When enabled, unit costs hide from proposal PDFs, coverage percentage labels display for each item, focus shifts to margin percentages, and sponsors see only total prices without cost breakdown.

### What happens if I don't configure coverage labels?

If you don't customize coverage labels, the system uses default labels: "Very Low Coverage" (0-25%), "Low Coverage" (25-50%), "Medium Coverage" (50-75%), "High Coverage" (75-100%), and "Full Coverage" (100%+). These defaults work for most organizations.

### Can sponsors see coverage percentages?

No, sponsors never see coverage percentages or labels. These are internal tools visible only to Package Builder users with Inventory permission. Proposals show only package name, tier, inclusions, quantities, and total sales price without any cost or margin information.

### How do I set target margins for my team?

Use coverage labels to communicate target margins. For example, rename "High Coverage" to "Target Range" for your preferred margin zone. Train team to aim for specific coverage percentage ranges, document margin policies in internal guidelines, and review packages before sending to ensure targets are met.

### What if my costs change after sending a proposal?

If costs change after sending a proposal, the proposal and any subsequent contract remain at the originally quoted price. Cost changes affect future packages only. Update unit costs in inventory settings, recalculate coverage for new packages, and consider whether to adjust pricing strategy based on new cost structure.
